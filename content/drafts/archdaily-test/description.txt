Mallya Aditi International School in Yelahanka, north Bangalore, was our first school. We won it through a design competition in 1990, and the original campus of load-bearing buildings, on a five-acre site, was planned for 600 children. It was among the first international schools in the city, and with few precedents to follow we started from the question of what a child's day at school should feel like.

Our answer was to leave out the corridor. Most schools of the period lined classrooms along long, dark, double-loaded passages. At Aditi the classrooms are instead arranged around a sequence of interconnected courtyards, each with its own scale and character. Every classroom opens onto one, so every room draws light from more than one side and is cross-ventilated, which in Bangalore's climate keeps it comfortable for most of the year without mechanical cooling. Moving between lessons means walking through open, planted space, and the courtyards became the school's circulation, its gathering places and its lungs.

Three decades later the school needed more space, and in 2018 we returned to design a first floor over the Junior Block. The challenge was structural as much as architectural: the existing building was thirty years old and load-bearing, and could not carry a conventional concrete storey. The new floor is therefore a lightweight steel structure. Its walls are built of lightweight Aerocon panels, and it is roofed with a combination of sheet and fabric roofs, keeping the added load to a minimum. The extension adds 1,708 square metres of classrooms, a library and counsellors' rooms, linked across the courtyards below by open galleries and a 1.5-metre-wide bridge.

The harder task was to make a new floor that still felt like Aditi. Light and air had been the core of the original design, and an upper storey risked shading the courtyards and closing them in. Rather than enclosing the new rooms, we kept the galleries open beneath the school's space-frame roofs, with glass balustrades that let light continue down into the courtyards. Clerestory windows and balconies recreate the quality of light and the ease of ventilation found in the classrooms on the ground floor.

The extension was completed in 2021. It shows that the idea at the heart of our first school, a place built around open, connected courtyards rather than corridors, could grow without being lost, and it was our chance to reinterpret that idea in a modern structure, thirty years on.
